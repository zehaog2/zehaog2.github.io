Boston Urban Heat Isand
Overview

This project quantifies the Boston urban heat island pattern and evaluates whether land-cover composition explains variation in land surface temperature, with a secondary environmental equity lens at the census-tract level.

Layer Transparency
Map 1: Land Surface Temperature

Shows classified surface temperature from the July 19, 2024 Landsat scene. Hottest zones concentrate in the dense urban core, establishing the baseline UHI pattern.

Map 2: Land Cover Classification

Displays NLCD classes with developed areas in warm tones and vegetation/water in cooler tones. This supports interpretation that land-cover composition explains thermal variation.

Map 3: NDVI Vegetation Index

NDVI ranges from near 0 (impervious) to near 1 (dense vegetation). Comparing with LST reveals an inverse vegetation-temperature relationship.

Map 4: Temperature Zone Classification

Reclassified LST into Cool (18-28C), Moderate (28-34C), and Hot (34-41C) zones to identify intervention-priority areas.

Map 5: Mean LST by Census Tract

Aggregates temperature to tract geography for policy framing; hotter tracts in Roxbury, Dorchester, and East Boston support an equity concern.

Key Methods
  • Landsat thermal conversion to Celsius: (B10 x 0.00341802) + 149.0 - 273.15
  • Zonal Statistics for mean LST by NLCD land-cover class
  • NDVI derivation: (B5 - B4) / (B5 + B4)
  • LST reclassification into Cool (18-28C), Moderate (28-34C), and Hot (34-41C) zones
  • Zonal Statistics for mean LST by census tract
  • CRS harmonization to NAD 1983 UTM Zone 19N

Technical Highlights
  • End-to-end ArcGIS Pro raster and vector workflow from ingestion through thematic outputs
  • Integrated remote sensing, land-cover, and tract-level socioeconomic datasets
  • Dual summarization strategy: class-level and tract-level thermal aggregation
  • Policy-oriented framing through spatially explicit heat-zone interpretation
  • Transparent treatment of methodological limits to protect claim quality
Limitations
  • Single-date satellite snapshot limits temporal generalization
  • 30 m raster resolution smooths micro-scale street heat variation
  • Income join issues constrain fully supported causal equity conclusions
