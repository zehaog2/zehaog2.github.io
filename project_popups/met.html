This project is a real-time urban temperature system focused on difficult, high-volatility weather regimes.

Problem and Value

On normal days, weather apps are usually good enough. But on messy, high-volatility days--when sea breeze timing, inversion breakup, cloud shifts, and local terrain effects all collide--the forecast can swing much more than a single number suggests. Getting those extremes right has real downstream value: better heat-risk planning for schools and outdoor workers, smarter utility load preparation during peak demand, better airport and city operations during rapid regime shifts, and more reliable timing for event organizers making go/no-go decisions.

Why Volatile-Day Forecasting Is Hard
  • Daily maximum temperature is highly sensitive to timing: sea-breeze onset, inversion erosion, cloud cover evolution, and terrain-driven flow shifts can each move outcomes by several degrees.
  • Urban effects and nearby water bodies add additional microclimate structure that broad models may not resolve at decision time scales.
  • When these processes overlap, small timing errors compound, and model spread often widens sharply.

Evidence Block A - Model Spread / IQR
  • San Francisco (KSFO) shows the widest spread in this run (about 8F), reflecting strong marine-layer and sea-breeze timing effects on daily maximum temperature.
  • Denver (KDEN) also shows elevated uncertainty (about 6F), consistent with mountainous terrain-driven variability and rapid boundary-layer transitions.
  • By contrast, a typical stable sunny setup in Philadelphia (KPHL) or Boston (KBOS) often clusters below 3F.
  • The starred focus models are updated dynamically each run using recent 7-day skill (RMSE and MAE), bias behavior, and known model strengths or weaknesses under the city’s active regime.
Evidence Block B - Volatile-Day Examples

KSFO - Mar 19, 2026

Regime: Sea-breeze mixing spike with thermal lag

  • WhatModelsMissed: Upside can persist into the wind-shift window; sea-breeze onset is not always immediate cooling.
  • ObservedSignals: Calm-period dip followed by wind-resumed warming, then a short spike with a sudden wind speed increase at frontal passage before marine cooling phase.
  • ForecastImpact: Observed max occurred near 5:05 PM local.
  • RuleUpdate: Treat transition windows explicitly and require cooling confirmation signals before declaring the daily maximum locked.

KDCA - Feb 18, 2026

Regime: Surface decoupling and consensus failure

  • WhatModelsMissed: Regional and consensus warmth can fail to realize at KDCA when local surface decoupling is active.
  • ObservedSignals: Inland neighbors stayed warmer while KDCA failed to converge under decoupling conditions.
  • ForecastImpact: Observed max occurred near 7:15 PM local.
  • RuleUpdate: Elevate local decoupling diagnostics to hard-stop risk controls, even when broader guidance clusters higher.
Technical Credibility
  • Modular architecture: risk calculator, data fetcher, and city modules separate concerns for traceability and maintainability.
  • Transparent local rules: station or city behavior is explicit in config and physics logic, with normalized-schema planning to keep outputs consistent as coverage grows.
  • Ensemble spread, trend behavior, and contextual diagnostics are surfaced alongside the forecast to quantify confidence and highlight regime risk.
Limitations and Next Steps
  • Current limitation: the project does not yet use a full historical archive calibration framework.
  • What is strong today: detailed event analysis, uncertainty diagnostics, and reliability controls that make reasoning auditable on difficult regimes.
  • Near-term next steps: continue targeted failure-regime research and convert those findings into explicit code and documentation updates.
