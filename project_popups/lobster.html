Overview

This case study models how water temperature shapes survival, growth, and developmental timing in American lobster (Homarus americanus) larvae from stage I through stage V, using open experimental data from the Gulf of Maine (Boothbay, 2021–2022). Rising temperatures in the Gulf motivate quantifying thermal thresholds and non-linear responses during the most vulnerable early life stages.

co-authored with Palak Tanwar

M1 — Survival classification

Binary prediction of molt vs. death from treatment temperature, developmental stage, diet, and rearing condition. Compared logistic regression, random forest, histogram gradient boosting, SVM (RBF), kNN, and decision trees with stratified 5-fold CV and a hold-out test set.

Selected model: Random Forest — highest AUC-ROC (0.899), strong precision (0.925), and stable cross-validation. Treatment temperature dominated feature importance (~74.6% Gini), with developmental stage second (~16.6%).

M2 — Time to molt (survivors)

Regression on TIME_UNTIL_MOLT for larvae that molted (N=330): linear regression, random forest, gradient boosting, and SVR (RBF). Metrics: RMSE, MAE, R².

Model RMSE (days) MAE (days) R²
Linear regression 13.96 8.18 0.454
Random Forest reg. 13.35 5.68 0.501
Gradient boosting reg. 13.30 5.67 0.504
SVR (RBF) 15.07 6.99 0.363

Best-performing regressor: gradient boosting (lowest MAE, highest R² ≈ 0.50). A large share of variance remains unexplained by the four predictors alone — consistent with batch, genetic, or unmeasured lab factors.

Dome-shaped thermal response: peak viability at 18°C; 30°C uniformly lethal in these trials; cold stress at 3°C yields very low molting success.

M4 — Thermal thresholds and growth

Dose–response view of survival vs. treatment temperature shows an asymmetric inverted-U peaking near 18°C, with a sharp drop between 26°C and 30°C. Among survivors, temperature and carapace length were weakly and non-significantly associated (Pearson r = 0.053, p = 0.351); one-way ANOVA found no significant temperature effect on carapace length (F = 1.390, p = 0.246) — temperature functions more as a survival gate than a size driver in this dataset.

Interpretation
  • Temperature is the primary survival driver.
  • Thermal extremes are asymmetric: acute high-temperature mortality vs. gradual cold stress.
  • Among survivors, carapace length did not differ significantly across temperatures (ANOVA p = 0.246) — temperature acts more as a viability filter than a size optimizer in this dataset.
