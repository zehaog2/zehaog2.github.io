Using text mining and sentiment analysis on 8,387 coffee review comments, we investigate a more specific question: what coffee features significantly boost the rating score of a professional reviewer?

The Data

Reviews from CoffeeReview.com span 1997–2025. Each review contains four comment categories:

  • Blind Assessment — sensory evaluation without knowing origin or brand
  • Notes — background on origin, variety, and processing
  • Who Should Drink It — recommended audience
  • Bottom Line — concise summary of overall impression
What Words Do Reviewers Reach For?

Top unigrams ranked by frequency, one panel per comment category. Sweet, chocolate, and fruit are the most prominent terms across all four.

Latent Topics: What Are Reviewers Really Talking About?

Latent Dirichlet Allocation (LDA) is a probabilistic topic model that treats each document as a mixture of latent topics, and each topic as a distribution over words. The beta value is the probability of a word given a topic — higher beta means the word is more characteristic of that topic.

Running LDA at k = 3, 5, and 7 topics, sweet, chocolate, and fruit are consistently the highest-beta words across all topic counts and all comment categories. The signal is stable regardless of model complexity.

Does Distinctive Language Hurt Ratings?

TF-IDF (Term Frequency–Inverse Document Frequency) measures how distinctive a word is to a specific review relative to all other reviews. A higher average TF-IDF indicates the reviewer is using more unique or uncommon language — departing from the shared vocabulary of the corpus.

All four comment categories show a negative correlation with rating. Reviewers tend to assign lower scores when their language is more distinctive.

Sentiment Tracks Quality — and History

Using Bing, AFINN, and NRC lexicons, net sentiment (positive minus negative word counts) shows a consistent positive correlation with rating across all three lexicons. More positively-worded reviews reliably accompany higher scores.

A single time series of annual average sentiment across all reviews. Two sharp drops are clearly visible:

  • 2008–2009 — the financial crisis
  • 2020–2021 — COVID-19

Reviewer sentiment fell in lockstep with broader societal stress. The practical implication: soliciting expert reviews during periods of economic or social disruption produces systematically lower scores — a confound worth controlling for in any longitudinal quality analysis.

Takeaway

Sweet, chocolate, and fruit are the vocabulary of quality. They appear in top word frequencies, dominant bigrams, stable LDA topics, and positive sentiment scores alike. If a coffee earns those descriptors, the rating follows. If a reviewer reaches for unusual language instead, the score likely won't.
